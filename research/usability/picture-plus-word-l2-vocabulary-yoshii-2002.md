# A word beside a picture helps learners keep the word

@trace spec:research-records

## Claim

Pairing a word with a picture helped adult language learners retain vocabulary
more than either alone, at least on immediate tests.

## Evidence

**Seen only through search summaries of the abstract; the article could not be
opened, so strength is capped at expert-opinion.**

151 adult ESL learners, beginning and intermediate, multimedia reading setting.
The combined group did best on immediate tests; delayed scores fell equally for
all groups. No interaction with proficiency. The listed year varies between 2002
and 2003 across catalogues.

Summaries of related work point the same way but less cleanly: Yoshii 2006 found
a picture advantage on definition supply but not recognition, and later critics
attribute the benefit to more attention rather than the pairing itself. Those
were not verified well enough to cite here.

These learners were reading, not working in a field, so incidental learning from
a task app is an inference. The evidence does not cover adults who cannot read in
any language. Adding audio of the word is a design choice.

## How it applies

- As per research:usability/picture-plus-word-l2-vocabulary-yoshii-2002, show the
  local-language word beside the task picture, using the same word every time.
- Where the word also appears in the worker's language, keep the local word
  visible so the screen still teaches it, a design choice.
- Treat vocabulary gain as a side benefit, not a promised outcome.
