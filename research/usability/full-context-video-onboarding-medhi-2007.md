# Show the whole situation on video, and let people rewatch it

@trace spec:research-records

## Claim

A dramatized video of why and how people use an application turned near-total
failure into completion for non-literate first-time users, but the effect faded.

## Evidence

Full text read from the Microsoft Research PDF. 35 non-literate residents of
Bangalore slums, on a text-free job-search PC application.

- Without video first: 1 of 17 finished, with 11 prompts in 8.2 min.
- With video first: 18 of 18 finished, averaging 4.7 prompts in 6.5 min.
- Same UI without video hours later: 7 of 18 (39%) finished, so 61% did not
  benefit; the authors suggest repeated viewing.

Users still needed prompts even with the video. This was a PC, not a phone, in a
small sample and a single study. The barriers it addresses (fear and mistrust,
not knowing what the machine offers) may differ for workers who own smartphones.
Using video for start/done is a design extrapolation.

## How it applies

- As per research:usability/full-context-video-onboarding-medhi-2007, onboarding
  can be a short video of a worker using the app in a field, in their language.
- Keep it replayable from the worker screen; do not show it once only.
- Consider showing the work being done in later task instructions, a design choice.
