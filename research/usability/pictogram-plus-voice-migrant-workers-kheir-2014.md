# Pictures need a voice: pictogram-only was the worst condition

@trace spec:research-records

## Claim

For low-literacy migrant workers, picture plus voice beat foreign-language text
plus voice. Picture alone came last.

## Evidence

**Seen only through search summaries of the abstract and repository record
(qspace.qu.edu.qa); the source itself could not be opened, so strength is capped
at expert-opinion per the library's rule.** The underlying work is a single
randomized study, and the closest population match found.

- 123 participants randomized to text + verbal, pictogram only, or pictogram +
  verbal; 11 of 23 screened label instructions were used.
- Some instructions stayed hard even with voice ("Do not take with dairy
  products", "Do not use by mouth"); the authors call for piloting with users.

Per-group percentages were not visible, so none are given. Medicine labels are
not farm tasks; applying this to a task app is an inference. The poor result for
those two prohibitions hints that "don't do X" is hard to picture, but the
summaries do not test that directly.

## How it applies

- As per research:usability/pictogram-plus-voice-migrant-workers-kheir-2014, a
  task icon is not enough on its own: pair it with a spoken instruction.
- This raises the priority of recorded audio: plan for it, and rely on the boss
  explaining in person until it ships.
- Pilot each task icon with actual workers.
- Avoid pictograms for prohibitions and caveats; say them in the recording.
