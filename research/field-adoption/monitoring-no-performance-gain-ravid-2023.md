# Monitoring workers does not buy performance

@trace spec:research-records

## Claim

Two independent meta-analyses find no dependable performance gain from electronic monitoring and
small costs to stress and satisfaction. Openness and low invasiveness help attitudes; they do not
remove the stress.

## Evidence

- Ravid et al.: 94 independent samples, N = 23,461. The abstract says organisations monitoring more
  transparently and less invasively can expect more positive attitudes, monitoring without an
  explicit purpose was not associated with performance increases, and people tend to find monitoring
  stressful whatever its characteristics. "No evidence that it improves performance" is the preprint
  and secondary wording; I could not check it in the published full text.
- Siegel et al.: 70 samples, 233 effect sizes. Job satisfaction r = -0.10, stress r = .11, performance
  r = -0.01, counterproductive behaviour r = .09. Moderator analyses suggest performance targets and
  feedback may worsen the negative effects. Lab studies probably understate field effects.
- Farm-specific evidence is thin. Wallace et al. followed 27 Latino men, H-2A farmworkers, wearing a
  health tracker for 90 days: community relationships helped adoption, and the vendor's device and app
  limited who could be recruited. One search summary also reported GPS surveillance worries being
  raised, with concern falling after consent (41% to 0%), but it called the table hard to read and a
  later search did not confirm it. Do not cite that number. The trackers were for the workers' health
  research, not employer monitoring, so trust there may not transfer.
- I found no study of migrant farmworkers' trust in employer task or GPS apps. This is a real gap.
- Neither meta-analysis is about farm labour; most samples will be other jobs.
- Related, weaker: Barankay's furniture-sales experiment (n=1754) found removing private rank feedback
  raised sales about 11%, in men only; it was a working paper when last listed. Bandiera, Barankay and
  Rasul (QJE 2005) found UK fruit pickers at least 50% more productive under piece rates than under
  relative pay. That is about pay, not a screen, so it is only an analogy.

## How it applies

- As per research:field-adoption/monitoring-no-performance-gain-ravid-2023, check GPS once at the tap
  on start and store a pass or fail; never record a track or poll location in the background.
- Tell workers in their language, in person, exactly what is recorded and why, and let them see it.
- No scores, rankings, minutes-per-task comparisons or speed targets. The purpose stays "did it get
  done", which is also the purpose that can be stated plainly.
